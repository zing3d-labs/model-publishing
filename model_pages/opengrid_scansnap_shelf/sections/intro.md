## Size

- **Assembled** — 280 × 280 × 56mm: a 10 × 2 tile span of grid, with 280mm of usable depth
- **Each half** — 140 wide × 280 deep × 56mm tall, which is what actually goes on the bed

It was sized around a **Fujitsu ScanSnap iX1600** (the iX1500 and iX1400 share that body), and anything of about that footprint will sit on it just as well. The scanner is roughly 292 × 161mm — very slightly wider than the deck, so it overhangs a few millimetres at each side, which is intended and changes nothing about how it sits. The depth is the generous direction: 161mm of scanner on 280mm of deck leaves room for the output tray to extend and for the cabling to sit behind.

If you are fitting something else, work from the deck: **280mm square**, with a back panel 56mm tall.

## Mounting

Each half's back panel has **10 openConnect positions**, in five columns of two, so 20 across the whole shelf. You do not need all of them — **4 per side is what this one is mounted with, and it is plenty sturdy.**

That said, this is a shelf you are going to put an expensive piece of equipment on, so use your own judgement about how much margin you want. Locking snaps are the right choice here: they are what stops the shelf lifting off its snaps, which matters a great deal more when something heavy is sitting on it. Add more than four a side if that is what makes you comfortable — the positions are there. **You are the one deciding your own tolerance for risk.**

**Any openConnect snap should work.** The plate included here has the Expanding Thread Snap, which is what this shelf is hung on, but the mount is a standard openConnect slot grid and nothing about it is fussy about which snap fills it.

## What You Need

- An openGrid wall or board — Full or Lite, either works — with at least a 10 × 2 tile clear span
- 8 openConnect snaps and 8 screws. **These are included on plate 3**, so there is nothing else to go and download
